# The Exchange — Companies as Players Concept Review

**Reviewed:** August 5, 2026  
**Related concept:** [[the-exchange-companies-concept]]

## Overall Assessment

The concept is strong because it gives players a reason to invest in and pay attention to one another—not merely react to board spaces. It could create alliances, rivalries, speculation, and more meaningful decisions between turns.

Because it changes a large part of the game's economy, it should first be introduced as a separate **Companies Mode** rather than immediately replacing the regular game. This would allow both versions to be tested and compared.

## Most Important Issue: Where Purchase Money Goes

The rules must explain where the money goes when someone purchases a player-company share.

If the purchase money goes to the company owner and increases that owner's pricing value, purchasing shares would immediately raise the share price. Players could artificially pump one another's company values.

### Recommended Starting Structure

- Every player company has 100 total shares.
- The owner permanently controls 60 founder shares.
- The bank begins with the remaining 40 public shares.
- Players initially purchase public shares from the bank.
- Later trades between players transfer money directly between the buyer and seller.
- Purchasing or selling a share does not directly change the issuing company's underlying value.

Using a 60/40 division gives the founder clear control and avoids a possible 50/50 ownership tie.

## Recommended Valuation Model

The proposed two-value system solves the circular-pricing problem. The two totals should be given simple, player-facing names.

### Company Value

Used to establish the price of a player's company shares:

> Company Value = cash + neutral company investments − loans − unpaid obligations

Shares held in other player-owned companies are excluded from this calculation. Therefore, one company's movement cannot bounce through several portfolios and return to its original company.

### Final Net Worth

Used to decide the winner:

> Final Net Worth = regular net worth + current value of shares held in other players' companies − outstanding debts

The owner should not add the value of their 60 founder shares to their score when their company's underlying cash and assets are already counted. Doing both would count the same wealth twice. The founder shares should represent control rather than an additional scored asset.

### Price Updates

Company share prices should be recalculated at the **end of the active player's turn**, rather than immediately after every small transaction. This makes price changes easier to understand and prevents the interface from displaying several rapid valuation changes during one turn.

Suggested safeguards:

- Establish a minimum share price, such as $25.
- Allow one rival to hold no more than 20 shares in another player's company.
- Allow a player to buy or sell no more than five player-company shares per turn.
- Let the bank buy shares at 90% of market value.
- Let the bank sell shares at 110% of market value.

These limits can be adjusted after playtesting.

## Right of First Refusal

The right-of-first-refusal rule is thematic and creates player interaction, but it could slow the game if every sale produces a long negotiation.

### Recommended Process

1. The seller chooses how many shares to sell.
2. The company owner receives one immediate opportunity to buy them at the displayed price.
3. If the owner passes, the seller may offer them to another player or sell them to the bank.
4. Private price negotiation is not allowed; the displayed market price is used.

This keeps the decision meaningful without interrupting the game's flow for too long.

## Insider Trading Recommendation

The risk-and-reward version fits the game best.

- An **Insider Tip** reveals the next movement affecting one stock or sector.
- The player may make one trade using that information.
- Using the information gives the player an **Investigation marker**.
- An Audit or SEC event can trigger a fine, a forced sale, or a temporary trading restriction for players with Investigation markers.

Example card text:

> **Insider Tip**  
> Your cousin's barber knows a guy in accounting. Totally legitimate.  
> Secretly view the next market event. You may make one trade, but take an Investigation marker if you use the information.

Company owners should not automatically know their own future price movement. That could make manipulation too easy.

## Trading Halt Recommendation

The repeated-doubles rule conflicts with the current rule that only allows one additional roll after doubles. A player may not have an opportunity to roll doubles three consecutive times during the same turn.

A shared **Market Heat meter** would work better:

- Every doubles roll adds one point of Market Heat.
- When Market Heat reaches three, a market-wide trading halt begins.
- Players may continue moving and resolving spaces.
- Players may pay obligations, but nobody may buy or sell shares.
- The halt ends at the beginning of the next round.
- Market Heat then resets to zero.

This makes the event uncommon while giving it a reasonable chance to appear during a full game.

## Player Companies Versus Board Companies

Player companies should be separate parent or holding companies rather than existing companies on the board.

Examples:

- Player A Capital
- Player B Holdings
- Player C Ventures

The existing board companies can remain neutral investments that use the current 12-step price ladder. This avoids confusion about whether a player, their company, or a board space is being purchased or taken over.

## Questions to Resolve Before Implementation

1. Do player-company shares pay dividends, or do investors profit only from price growth?
2. What happens when a player company's value reaches zero?
3. Can player-company shares be traded during any turn or only after landing on certain spaces?
4. Does taking over a neutral board company immediately increase Company Value?
5. Does the bank hold public shares permanently, or can the owner eventually repurchase and retire them?
6. What information about another player's company is visible before buying its shares?

At minimum, a company card should display:

- Current share price
- Previous share price and direction
- Player's average purchase price
- Shares owned
- Unrealized gain or loss
- Company Value
- Debt and unpaid obligations
- Public shares still available

## Suggested Prototype Order

The first version should test only the core economy:

1. Give every player a separate company.
2. Use 100 shares with a 60/40 founder-to-public split.
3. Calculate share price from Company Value.
4. Allow players to buy and sell public shares.
5. Display gain and loss for every investment.
6. Include the new holdings in the final score.

After this version is balanced, add the right of first refusal, Insider Tips, Market Heat, trading halts, and any player-company dividend system.

## Final Recommendation

This concept is worth prototyping. Its strongest feature is that another player's success becomes both a threat and a possible investment opportunity.

The system should begin as an optional **Companies Mode**, using clear bank-held public shares and a valuation calculation that excludes cross-holdings. The initial playtest should focus on whether investing in other players is understandable, profitable, and competitive before adding the more complicated event mechanics.
